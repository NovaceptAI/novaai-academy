01 / JUNIOR COHORT

AI Explorers

Class 8-10 | Proposed: 6 weeks, 15 live hours | About 1 hour of independent practice weekly. Basic computer use; no coding prerequisite.

01 / Meet AI

Session A: Models, predictions, everyday examples and limitations.

Session B: Investigate correct, misleading and invented responses; introduce privacy.

Evidence of learning: An AI detective notebook with verified examples.

Practice: Find two everyday AI uses and describe one limitation of each.

02 / Ask better questions

Session A: Instructions, context, examples and output formats.

Session B: Revise a vague prompt; compare outputs against a simple checklist.

Evidence of learning: A prompt collection showing before-and-after improvements.

Practice: Improve three prompts and explain why the revisions helped.

03 / Research responsibly

Session A: Reliable sources, checking claims and uncertainty.

Session B: Build a short presentation using sources students can locate and check.

Evidence of learning: A source-backed presentation that separates evidence from AI suggestions.

Practice: Verify three claims and record their sources.

04 / Create with purpose

Session A: Storytelling, visual briefs, permission and attribution.

Session B: Create a poster or illustrated explainer for a chosen community topic.

Evidence of learning: A creative output with a note explaining tools, choices and checks.

Practice: Revise the work using feedback from one reader.

05 / Build a small website

Session A: HTML/CSS concepts using a starter template: files, links and layout.

Session B: Modify the template with AI assistance; test links, readability and claims.

Evidence of learning: A working educational or personal-interest webpage.

Practice: Prepare the final project and a short explanation of how it works.

06 / Finish and explain

Session A: Capstone workshop: scope, testing and revisions.

Session B: Student showcase with questions and parent feedback.

Evidence of learning: Final project, demonstration and reflection.

Practice: Write what worked, what failed and what to try next.

Capstone options: An educational mini-site, a verified study resource, or a community campaign with a webpage and poster. Assessment focuses on understanding and responsible creation; complex integrations are not required.

02 / SENIOR COHORT

AI Builders

Class 11-12 | Proposed: 8 weeks, 24 live hours | About 1-2 hours of independent practice weekly. Basic computer use; no prior coding required.

01 / AI literacy and judgement

Session A: Models, limitations, privacy and academic integrity.

Session B: Compare outputs and apply a simple quality checklist.

Evidence of learning: An AI evaluation worksheet with explanations.

Practice: Collect examples of a useful answer and an unreliable one.

02 / Structured prompting

Session A: Break a problem into steps; define requirements and acceptance criteria.

Session B: Test a reusable prompt across multiple inputs and inspect failure cases.

Evidence of learning: A tested workflow with a small example set.

Practice: Revise the workflow after identifying at least one failure.

03 / Research and data

Session A: Source verification, spreadsheet organisation and data quality.

Session B: Clean a small dataset, create charts and distinguish patterns from unsupported conclusions.

Evidence of learning: An evidence-based dashboard and short written interpretation.

Practice: Check labels, missing values and the accuracy of one conclusion.

04 / Web foundations

Session A: HTML, CSS and essential JavaScript through a guided starter.

Session B: Inputs, events and simple application logic.

Evidence of learning: A functioning calculator, quiz or revision tool.

Practice: Modify one feature and explain the event-to-output sequence.

Midpoint checkpoint

Students demonstrate their small app, explain the important code and show one test. If foundations are weak, simplify the capstone and revisit the starter before adding integrations.

Working habits

Save versions, record bugs, ask specific questions and disclose AI assistance. A generated solution must be read, tested and explained before it is accepted.

02 / SENIOR COHORT

From prototype to project

AI Builders | Weeks 5-8

05 / AI-assisted coding

Session A: Read generated code, break requests into small changes and debug errors.

Session B: Use test cases to improve behaviour rather than repeatedly regenerating the app.

Evidence of learning: A revised application and test log.

Practice: Reproduce and fix one bug; explain its cause.

06 / APIs and automation

Session A: Requests, responses, JSON, secrets and usage costs.

Session B: Connect one suitable public API or instructor-managed AI endpoint.

Evidence of learning: A functioning integration with an error state.

Practice: Test missing input, an unsuccessful request and a normal response.

07 / Capstone workshop

Session A: Define the user need, scope and implementation tasks.

Session B: Build the main workflow and test it with another learner.

Evidence of learning: A working draft with a prioritised improvement list.

Practice: Resolve the most important issue and prepare the presentation.

08 / Release and present

Session A: Deployment, accessibility, source checks and final testing.

Session B: Demonstrate the application and answer questions about its behaviour.

Evidence of learning: A deployed app and concise project report.

Practice: Document limitations and a sensible future improvement.

Capstone options

A revision quiz with explanations and sources; a study planner with editable tasks; a public-data explorer; or an organiser for student-supplied research notes.

Final handover

A working application or shareable build, source files, a short README, test cases, AI-use disclosure and a demonstration. Live AI is optional: it should serve the project rather than be added only for the label.

03 / UNIVERSITY COHORT

AI Innovators

Undergraduate and postgraduate students | Proposed: 10 weeks, 30 live hours | About 2-3 hours of independent practice weekly.

Readiness: A short diagnostic checks basic coding, functions, data structures and debugging. Learners needing these foundations complete a preparation pack before joining; the course is not a substitute for a full beginner programming course.

01 / Problem selection

Session A: Suitable AI use cases, model limitations and deterministic alternatives.

Session B: Define a user, baseline, narrow scope and measurable success criteria.

Evidence of learning: A one-page problem brief.

Practice: Collect permitted example inputs and define expected behaviour.

02 / Engineering essentials

Session A: Review Python functions, collections, JSON, files and error handling.

Session B: Git, environments, debugging and a small data-processing exercise.

Evidence of learning: A reproducible repository with setup instructions.

Practice: Run the project from a clean environment and improve the README.

03 / Model APIs

Session A: Model requests, structured outputs, validation and secret handling.

Session B: Implement a model call with failure handling and basic usage logging.

Evidence of learning: A small AI service.

Practice: Test invalid input, invalid output and a failed request.

04 / Evaluation before expansion

Session A: Create a test dataset and define task-specific quality criteria.

Session B: Compare a baseline with revised prompts and inspect errors.

Evidence of learning: An evaluation report with examples and limitations.

Practice: Improve one recurring failure and rerun the same tests.

05 / Retrieval fundamentals

Session A: Documents, chunks, embeddings and search quality.

Session B: Build a small retrieval pipeline with visible source references.

Evidence of learning: A searchable collection using public, synthetic or permitted data.

Practice: Test whether relevant passages are retrieved for sample questions.

03 / UNIVERSITY COHORT

From evidence to deployment

AI Innovators | Weeks 6-10

06 / Grounded applications

Session A: Generate answers from retrieved evidence; handle insufficient evidence.

Session B: Test irrelevant questions, missing information and misleading document instructions.

Evidence of learning: A grounded assistant prototype.

Practice: Record supported, unsupported and incorrectly sourced answers.

07 / Controlled workflows

Session A: Tool calling, deterministic steps, validation and human approval.

Session B: Connect one bounded tool and handle errors without unintended actions.

Evidence of learning: An auditable workflow.

Practice: Test invalid arguments and confirm approval is required where designed.

08 / Interface and deployment

Session A: Create a lightweight UI with loading, empty and error states.

Session B: Deploy with server-side secrets, usage limits and basic logs.

Evidence of learning: An accessible deployed prototype.

Practice: Check setup reproducibility and test the deployed main workflow.

09 / Capstone hardening

Session A: User testing, failure analysis and scope reduction.

Session B: Improve quality, latency and cost; document known limitations.

Evidence of learning: A release candidate with evaluation results.

Practice: Complete the handover documentation and demo rehearsal.

10 / Demonstration and handover

Session A: Final evaluation, documentation and reproducibility checks.

Session B: Product demonstration and technical questioning.

Evidence of learning: Application, repository, evaluation report and handover notes.

Practice: Identify the next improvement supported by test evidence.

Capstone options: A document assistant with references, a ticket classifier with human review, a research organiser grounded in supplied documents, or a content-drafting workflow with approval before publication. Use one provider and a lightweight interface initially.

04 / LEARNING AGREEMENT

Prepare. Practise. Explain.

What students and parents should know before enrolment.

Preparation and access

Learners need access to a laptop or desktop, a modern browser, reliable internet for practice and permission to install the agreed tools where needed. Phone-only participation is not suitable for the building exercises. Exact specifications and tool costs will be shared before enrolment. Tool choices will be checked for current age eligibility and account requirements. Parent or guardian approval is required for minors. Instructor demonstrations will be used where individual accounts are unsuitable. Students must not upload sensitive personal or client information.

A typical class

Activity Explorers: 75 min Builders / Innovators: 90 min
Recap 5 minutes 5 minutes
Concept 15 minutes 20 minutes
Demonstration 10 minutes 15 minutes
Guided building 35 minutes 40 minutes
Review 10 minutes 10 minutes

Assessment

Understanding and explanation: 25% | Functionality and usefulness: 25% | Testing, evidence and responsible use: 25% | Iteration and documentation: 15% | Presentation: 10%. Students disclose their AI assistance and explain their work. Final projects are evaluated at the appropriate cohort level. Completion criteria and any academy-issued certificate arrangements will be confirmed before enrolment; no external accreditation, job placement or income outcome is implied.

Before you reserve a place

Confirm the final dates, teaching format, batch size, venue, fee, payment schedule, included tools, absence policy and cancellation/refund terms. This syllabus describes the proposed learning plan and does not collect payment or guarantee a seat.
